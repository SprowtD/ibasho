# Paneler checklist (sources: akirao3wq 20-page study, manga-page PAGE_STYLE.md, scene-planning craft notes)

Story and pacing
1. Every page ends on a beat: a reaction, question, reveal or punchline. Write it as the page `note`.
2. Page 1 works alone in a feed: one strong image and a line that makes sense without context.
3. Reveals sit on the page turn (the first panel of the next page), never mid-page.
4. Running gags and callbacks are placed on purpose (e.g. "my peace is over" bookends).

Panels
5. 3–6 panels a page (hard limit 6), about 5 in 3 tiers as the plain page (2, 1, 2 is the commonest real one: vary from it); one panel clearly heavier than the rest (the dominant panel). A page of 1–2 for a big moment.
6. No two pages in a row on the same grid.
7. Background strength follows the panel's job, so a page has strong and weak panels: the panel that matters most (establishing shot, punchline, turn) gets a full background, talk panels a hint, reactions and in-between panels tone or white. Never every panel of a page on the same level; at most one or two full on a page.
8. Inserts of hands, feet, eyes and props carry emotion without words.
8a. The biggest moment may be a splash panel (`"splash": true`): the dominant panel, half the page or more, one bold image. Rare: at most one in a short chapter.
9. Wide thin strips are for timing (silence, eyes opening, a pause).
10. One point-of-view panel for the key moment of a scene.
11. Flashbacks look different (heavy black frame, soft). Heavy black only on a few key panels.
12. Time of day moves through the chapter and shows in the backgrounds.
13. Pool (a series about the game): table shots as top-down diagrams with numbered balls, staged in 3D first; count pockets.

Words
14. Every script line once, whole, word for word, in order. Long lines split into joined balloons at a pause.
15. 35–55 lettered words a page (limit 70); at most 5 spoken lines a page, otherwise add a page; at most 3 balloons a panel, otherwise add a panel.
16. A speaker is in the frame, or the line is marked off-panel. Use the script's quiet/loud notes to pick whisper or shout.

Panel director (19 Nov 2026; references A, B, H, J)
17. Every panel has a job: establish (sets the place), talk, act (something happens), react, insert (a detail), big (the big moment). If you cannot say what a panel is for, cut it or merge it.
18. Every panel has a weight, 1 to 5, from the story: the opening, a change of feeling and the big moment are heavy; in-between panels are light. One panel a page is clearly the heaviest; at least two are light. Never all the same: that is an even grid (2x2, 2x3, 3x3), and it reads like a slideshow.
19. One big panel every two pages: one person, from the chest up, one short line (six to eight words).
20. Two panels side by side never share both camera distance and angle. Move through long, middle and close; high and low.
21. Upright gutters do not line up from one tier to the next, and no two pages in a row share a layout (the builder sees to both when it picks the layout).

Manga flow (references G, H)
22. Sides stay put: within a scene each person keeps their side of the picture, and faces the other across the gutter, until a wide panel shows them move. The first speaker stands on the side that is read first.
23. One big expression a page at most (`"face": "big"`). Calm faces everywhere else.
24. Density changes: the establishing panel and the big moment are drawn fully; connecting panels (talk, react, insert) are a face or a hand on white or tone, with NO background.
25. Silent panels are wanted: a look, a hand, "…" between two lines. A page of talk has one.
26. Short balloons, a few words each. A line over about 14 words is split at a pause (`parts`) or said over two panels before it is squeezed.

Story and page turn (references K, L). Advice to the author; a book's own words are never changed.
27. The panel that surprises is the FIRST panel of its page (`"reveal": true`); the last panel of the page before sets it up with a question, a look, a hand reaching.
28. The first five pages of a story carry fewer words (45 a page at most) and are easy to read: the opening is where the reader is won or lost.
29. The turn of the story gets the biggest picture in the chapter (the splash; a full page when it earns it).
30. When the picture says it, the line can go: a crying face does not need "I am so sad". In an original script, flag it for the author; in a book, keep the line.
